# The RICH Economy, and why this repository is shaped the way it is

I borrowed the shape of this repository from this idea. This is a summary. I have tried to say where each piece comes from.

## The source

Robert Anton Wilson published an essay called "The RICH Economy" in *The Illuminati Papers* (1980). RICH stands for Rising Income through Cybernetic Homeostasis. He credits the scheme to the inventor L. Wayne Benner, worked out with Wilson. He builds it on three older proposals. C. H. Douglas's National Dividend, later taken up by Ezra Pound and Buckminster Fuller. Robert Theobald's Guaranteed Annual Income. Milton Friedman's Negative Income Tax. He cites Norbert Wiener for seeing in 1947 that cybernetics would mean mass unemployment. He cites Aristotle for the point that slavery ends only when the machines can run themselves.

Wilson then wrote the scheme into his fiction. The *Schrödinger's Cat* trilogy starts with *The Universe Next Door* (1979). In one of its parallel universes a President Hubbard enacts the whole thing.

## The four stages, from the essay

1. Accept that automation and the unemployment it brings are coming, and speed them up. The method is a reward, 100,000 dollars in the essay, to any worker who can design a machine that will replace him or her and everyone else doing the same work.
2. Put a floor under everyone with a Negative Income Tax or a Guaranteed Annual Income. The unemployment from stage one then does not turn into poverty.
3. Raise that floor gradually, perhaps 5 percent a year for the first ten years, until it reaches the level of a National Dividend. Every citizen is treated as a shareholder in what the country produces.
4. Invest heavily in adult education. People with free time and nothing to do with it get bored, and there is larger work waiting once the drudgery is gone.

The argument underneath is simple. Most jobs are a nuisance that people do for the paycheck. The paycheck is what people need. The two can be separated. Wilson says the job is the disease and unemployment is the cure. Afterward he expects a "Work Esthetic" in place of the Work Ethic. People work because they want to tinker, explore and make things.

## The version in the novels

In the fiction the reward is a salary. The sentence usually quoted says President Hubbard's first step in establishing the RICH Economy was to offer a prize of 50,000 dollars per year to any worker who could design a machine that would replace him or her. Hubbard also declares every citizen a shareholder and pays out a National Dividend each year.

I could not get the full passage from the novels in front of me while writing this. I have only repeated the parts I could check against a source. The summaries I found place the robot workforce material in the second volume, *The Trick Top Hat*, as well as the first.

## The part that matters here

Stage one makes a claim about knowledge. I think it is correct, and it has nothing to do with economics. The person who can design the machine that replaces a job is the person who has done the job. They know which steps are real and which are ritual. They know what breaks, what the customer is paying for, and what must never be done without asking. Someone who has not done the job can copy the motions. They cannot write the specification.

So the unit of this repository is a job description written for a machine. Each project folder starts with a `JOB.md`. It says what a person used to do by hand, why it is hard, what the machine does now, what still needs a person, and how to rebuild it. Where I have published the source code it is the second thing in the folder, and the less important one.

## What is missing

Wilson's scheme has a payer. The worker who defines the job well enough for a machine gets paid for it, and everyone displaced gets a rising floor. Neither exists. Nobody is offering the prize and there is no dividend. Describing the job is the one part a single worker can do alone. That is the part I am doing.
